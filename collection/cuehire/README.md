# cuehire.cuesoft.io: Talent and Project Management Agency

## Contact form

- **What:** name, work email, company, your industry, the kind of help you
  want (end-to-end delivery, managed engineers, referral hiring or not sure
  yet), and your brief. We also record the
  country Cloudflare’s edge places you in: derived from your IP address as
  you submit rather than asked for on the form, and stored as the country
  name, not the address itself. It tells us where an enquiry is coming from
  when we scope a response.
- **Why:** to respond to your enquiry and scope a potential engagement.
- **Where:** records are stored in Airtable, our forms database, and the
  brief is also sent to the Cuesoft platform that runs the signed-in portal
  below, which holds it so the team can answer it.
- **Legal basis:** our legitimate interest in responding to and scoping
  the business enquiry you sent us (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)): you may
  [object](../../rights/your-rights/) at any time. Once an engagement is
  signed, processing under it rests on the contract (Art. 6(1)(b)).
- **Confirmation:** we email you a receipt, copied to
  [cuehire@cuesoft.io](mailto:cuehire@cuesoft.io): see
  [what every site collects](../all-sites/).

Engagement data under a signed agreement is governed by that agreement’s
data terms.

- **What they receive:** a cookie or device identifier, your IP address,
  the page URLs you visit on this site and the referring URL, plus basic
  device and browser metadata and the page-view events themselves. Google
  Analytics uses the IP address in passing to derive an approximate
  location and then discards it: the address itself is not stored, and the
  location is what is kept. Google Analytics keeps
  this for **14 months**: see [retention](../../handling/retention/). We
  do not send your form contents to any of them.
- **One event beyond page views:**
  when you send us a brief,
  **Google Ads, Meta, LinkedIn and X** are told that it happened, so
  they can credit the ad you arrived from. Google Analytics is not: it
  receives the page views described above and nothing more. The event
  records the fact of the brief and nothing else: not your name, not
  your email, not your phone number, and not what you wrote.
- **If you are signed in to Google:** we have Google signals switched on,
  so where you are signed in to a Google account that has Ads
  Personalisation enabled, Google may connect this visit to that account
  and combine it with what Google already holds there: location, search
  history, YouTube history and activity on sites that partner with Google.
  What that does, precisely: it lets us see age, gender and interest
  summaries, and it lets Google build advertising audiences that can reach
  you across your devices. It does **not** merge your devices together in
  our own reports (since February 2024 Google signals is not part of how
  Analytics counts users), so we do not see your phone and your laptop as
  one person. And Google uses the visit for its own ads personalisation as
  well as for our measurement. You control it on Google’s side,
  independently of us, at
  [myadcenter.google.com](https://myadcenter.google.com) and
  [Google’s activity controls](https://myaccount.google.com/activitycontrols):
  switching Ads Personalisation off there stops it for every site, not
  just ours. Declining our banner prevents it entirely.
- **Decline and none of them load.** The site works identically either
  way.
- **Withdraw any time** via the site’s **Cookie preferences** link.
  Withdrawal stops the tags loading from that point on; it does not delete

  to data already collected differs by provider: Google Analytics holds it
  as our **processor**, on our instructions, so the
  [rights page](../../rights/your-rights/) reaches it. The exception is what
  Google signals feeds into Google’s own ads personalisation, which Google
  controls rather than us: manage that in your
  [Google account](https://myadcenter.google.com). The four advertising
  platforms hold what they collected as **independent businesses** under
  their own policies: see
  [processors and platforms](../../handling/processors/).
- An opt-out preference signal (**Global Privacy Control**) overrides a
  stored opt-in for as long as your browser sends it.
- **Legal basis:** consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)); California treatment is on
  the [United States page](../../jurisdictions/united-states/).

## Performance and error monitoring

This site loads **Datadog**’s browser monitoring. It is operational
telemetry, so it does not wait for the consent banner, and declining the
banner does not stop it: it measures how quickly a page loads and records
the errors it hits, so that we can keep the pages working.

- **What Datadog receives:** the page addresses you visit and the page
  that referred you, load and resource timings, your clicks (which
  element, never anything you type), script errors and failed requests,
  your browser, device and operating system, and your IP address, from
  which it derives an approximate location. Nothing links a session to
  your name or email. No session is recorded as a replay, with one
  exception: if a page fails to render correctly, Datadog records that
  session as a replay so we can see what went wrong. A replay shows the
  page as it appeared to you, and the contents of form fields are masked.
- **Cookie:** one first-party session cookie, `_dd_s_v2`, holding a random
  session identifier that lapses after 15 minutes of inactivity (4 hours
  at most).
- **Role and retention:** Datadog acts as our **processor**, on our
  instructions, and keeps the data in the United States for no more than
  **30 days**, under the safeguards on the
  [transfers page](../../handling/transfers/); see
  [processors and platforms](../../handling/processors/) and
  [retention](../../handling/retention/).
- **Legal basis:** our legitimate interest in keeping the pages working
  (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)); you may [object](../../rights/your-rights/) at any
  time.

## The Signed-In Portal

Clients and talent who work with CueHIRE™ sign in at
[account.cuesoft.io](../account/#signing-up-and-signing-in) and come back
here to a portal. This site holds none of it: every page reads from and
writes to the Cuesoft platform that runs your account, which holds it. The
platform answers only for the signed-in person, and the portal you see depends
on your account: a client sees the projects, invoices and payments of its
own company, and talent sees their own contracts, hours and pay. Each part
below names what it collects or shows, why, where it is held, who receives
it and the legal basis. Apart from Paystack for card payments (see
[Paying an Invoice](#paying-an-invoice)) and the monitoring and tags
described on this page, no outside service receives anything from the
portal.

- **Where:** everything in the portal is held on the Cuesoft platform that
  runs your account.
- **Legal basis:** performing the contract between you or your company and
  Cuesoft (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b), and the equivalent contractual basis under the
  NDPA), and our legitimate interest in running and securing the portal
  (Art. 6(1)(f)): you may [object](../../rights/your-rights/) at any time.
  Where a signed agreement sets its own data terms, that agreement governs.

### Overview

The overview shows your name, the projects, invoices and hours behind your
account and the totals drawn from them. It collects nothing of its own. Why: so you
see where your work and payments stand.

### Projects (Clients and Talent)

- **Shows:** each project’s name, type, status, dates, summary, progress,
  milestones with their due dates, the live address and code repository the
  project names, and the team on it: each member’s name, role and start date.
  A client sees the projects it is the customer on; talent sees the projects
  they are placed on, with their own allocation, role, rate and dates, and the
  pull requests (title, number, state, dates and the GitHub username of the
  author) and daily commit counts the platform has read from the project’s
  repository.
- **Collects:** nothing on these pages beyond the hours described below.
- **Why:** so clients follow delivery and talent see their own work.
- **Who receives it:** the people on the other side of the engagement see
  what is described here: a client sees the names and roles of the team
  placed on its project.

### Contracts and Signing

- **Shows:** the contracts issued to you: title, type, date issued, status
  (awaiting signature or signed), the clauses and a download link.
- **Collects:** when you sign, the full name you type. The platform records
  that name, the time and the IP address you signed from, once for each
  contract.
- **Why:** to record that you accepted the contract, and when.
- **Who receives it:** Cuesoft.

### Documents

- **Shows:** the documents Cuesoft has shared with you: title, type, file
  name, size, date and a download link.
- **Collects:** nothing: you download, you do not upload, here.
- **Why:** so you hold the paperwork of the engagement.

### Hours (Talent)

- **Collects:** each entry you log: the project, the date worked, the number
  of hours and a description. The platform adds the rate and amount and holds
  the entry’s status (submitted, approved or rejected) and the reviewer’s
  note.
- **Shows:** your entries, their status and your totals.
- **Why:** to bill for the work and to pay you for it.
- **Who receives it:** Cuesoft’s staff review each entry.

### Paystubs and Payout Details (Talent)

- **Shows:** your paystubs as documents you can download.
- **Collects:** the bank details your pay is sent to (account name, account
  number, bank, sort code and currency) and the work history you keep on your
  account, which you enter and edit on
  [account.cuesoft.io](../account/#payout) rather than here; the portal
  reads them back, with the account number masked, and shows whether staff
  have verified the account.
- **Why:** to pay you.

### Invoices and Receipts (Clients)

- **Shows:** each invoice: number, project, dates, status, amounts, line items,
  tax and discount, notes, the instalment schedule, the payments and
  transfers made against it, and a PDF. Each receipt: number, date, amount,
  method and reference, with a PDF.
- **Collects:** nothing on these pages beyond what the next sections describe.
- **Why:** so you can see what you owe and what you have paid, and keep proof
  of it.
- **Who receives it:** Cuesoft’s staff, who issue the invoice and the
  receipt.

### Payment Plan (Clients)

- **Collects:** the plan you choose for an invoice, from the plans Cuesoft
  offers (a deposit and instalments, or one payment).
- **Why:** to split an invoice into instalments you agreed to.

### Paying an Invoice

CueHIRE™ has no card form of its own. This site has no field for a card
number, expiry date or security code, never sees them and never sends them
to the Cuesoft platform; the Cuesoft platform also refuses a card number if
one ever reached it.

- **Pay by card:** you press Pay and are sent to **Paystack’s own hosted
  payment page**, where the card is typed. Before that, the site asks the
  platform to start the payment, sending the amount, the currency, **your
  email address**, the address to return you to and the invoice and
  instalment it is for; the platform passes the email address and the amount
  to Paystack and gives the site back a reference. When you return, the site
  sends the platform only that reference, and the platform asks Paystack
  whether the payment succeeded and records the payment and its receipt.
  Paystack receives the card from you on its page, and your email address and
  the amount from the platform, under its own security certification and
  [privacy notice](https://paystack.com/privacy): see
  [processors and platforms](../../handling/processors/).
- **Cards you save:** the Pay by Card page lists the cards on your account
  (brand, last four digits, expiry and whether it is the default) and links
  to **Manage Cards in Account**, the signed-in
  [Cards section of the account page](../account/#cards). Adding a card opens
  Paystack’s own window over the page, loaded only when you press Add a
  Card; the platform starts a card verification of 50 naira with your email
  address, refunds it once the card is saved, and keeps the card’s brand,
  last four digits, expiry and Paystack’s token for it, never the number or
  security code. What it sends, what it keeps and the refund are set out on
  the account page.
- **Pay by bank transfer:** the page shows Cuesoft’s bank details and the
  reference to quote. You then declare the transfer: the transfer reference,
  an optional note, and a proof-of-payment file (up to 10 MB), which the
  platform keeps in its file storage with the amount, currency, invoice and
  instalment, and your account. Cuesoft’s staff review the declaration and
  confirm or reject it with a note, which you see.
- **Why:** to take the payments you choose to make and record them against
  the invoice.
- **Who receives it:** Paystack for card payments, and Cuesoft’s staff for
  transfers.
- **Legal basis:** performing the contract under which you pay (GDPR
  Art. 6(1)(b), and the equivalent contractual basis under the NDPA).

[performance and error monitoring](#performance-and-error-monitoring) above, nothing
loads before you accept, and the cookieless Cloudflare measurement described
in [what every site collects](../all-sites/) stays outside the consent gate
because it sets no cookie.
